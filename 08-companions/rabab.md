## Letter — *The Woman Who Would Not Remarry*

<!-- LETTER START -->
● Rabab was married to Imam Husayn, and their daughter Sakina was with them both, a small child in the household, well before any of what came later.

○ What happened to her after Karbala?

● She survived it, and lived on afterward for a good number of years, by most accounts never remarrying, even though remarrying would have been the expected, entirely practical thing for her to do.

○ Why wouldn't she remarry, though?

● The accounts don't record her explaining herself in any real detail. They simply record the plain fact of it, repeated so consistently across the different sources that it has become one of the things she is remembered for specifically.

○ Did people expect her to remarry quickly?

● They did, generally — it was the practical and the expected path for a widow in her position, and most people around her would not have thought less of her for taking it.

○ Isn't grieving for that long unusual?

● It is unusual, which is exactly why people kept retelling it — a kind of loyalty that didn't need an audience, or an occasion, or anyone watching. It just continued, quietly, year after year, on its own.

○ Could you keep a feeling that steady, for that long?

● Most people's grief changes shape over the years, even when the loss itself doesn't get any smaller. Rabab's, by the accounts that survive, mostly didn't change at all.

○ Have you felt something that steady, for that long? Say it, out loud.

● Most people have at least one thing exactly like that, even if they don't talk about it very often.

●○ She didn't remarry, and the accounts don't ask her to explain why.
<!-- LETTER END -->

## Fact panel — letter reverse

### RABAB BINT IMRA' AL-QAYS

● **Her marriage.** Married to Imam Husayn; mother of Sakina, who was a young child in the household before Karbala.

● **What is recorded of her afterward.** By most accounts, she did not remarry after Karbala, and continued mourning for the remainder of her life.

● **Why this is notable.** Remarriage was the ordinary, expected course; the consistency of the account across sources is itself part of why it is remembered.

● **What the accounts don't do.** Explain her reasoning. They record the fact and stop, rather than narrating her private grief.

● **Why she is in this envelope.** *Rabab was Imam Husayn's wife, and by most accounts never remarried or stopped mourning him for the rest of her life.*

*Traditionally told; the specific span of her later life varies across accounts.*
