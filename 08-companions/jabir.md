## Letter — *The Greeting That Took Sixty Years*

<!-- LETTER START -->
● There was a very old man named Jabir who, as a much younger man, had spent real time close to the Prophet — one of the people who had actually walked beside him and remembered exactly how he talked.

○ Was that unusual?

● Not by itself — plenty of people had known the Prophet. What made Jabir unusual is that he lived long enough afterward to still be doing something about it, decades on.

○ How old was he by then?

● Very old. Old enough that his eyes had mostly gone, and people had to lead him by the arm to get anywhere at all.

○ Where did he want to go?

● To a particular house, to find a particular boy, because of something the Prophet had told him to do a very long time before — before that boy's own father had even been born yet.

○ What had the Prophet told him to do?

● That one day, a child in his family would be born and named Muhammad al-Baqir, and that when Jabir eventually met him, he was to pass on a greeting, in the Prophet's own name.

○ So he just waited, his whole life, for that?

● He waited decades, without knowing when it would happen or whether he would even live to see it happen at all. And when word finally reached him, old and nearly blind by then, that a boy of that name had been born into that family, he had someone walk him there himself, straight away.

○ Did he find him?

● He found him, and gave him the greeting exactly as it had been given to him, all those years before, word for word, with nothing changed.

○ Could you remember exact words for sixty years? Say one thing out loud.

● Some things you hold onto precisely because you already know, from the very day you're given them, that you're going to need them again someday — you just don't know when that day will be.

●○ Sixty years, carried word for word, and delivered right on time.
<!-- LETTER END -->

## Fact panel — letter reverse

### JABIR IBN ABDULLAH AL-ANSARI

● **Who he was.** A companion who had spent real time in the Prophet's company, from Medina, remembered for both his long life and his memory.

● **What he carried.** A greeting from the Prophet, to be delivered to a descendant not yet born — Muhammad al-Baqir, the fifth Imam — named to Jabir decades before that child existed.

● **How long he waited.** Most of his own remaining lifetime, into old age, without knowing when or whether the moment would come.

● **How he delivered it.** In person, blind by then, led to the house, and gave the greeting in the exact words he had first received it in.

● **Why he is in this envelope.** *Jabir carried a greeting from the Prophet to Imam al-Baqir, delivered decades after it was given.*

He died in Medina, an old man, not long after.

## Notes

**This is the companions-line entry for Imam al-Baqir (05)**, the first of the later Imams to get one — every companion so far had pointed only to the Prophet or Imam Ali. Worth treating as the template for how the remaining, currently uncovered Imams could each get one in turn.

**The letter ends on delivery, not on Jabir's own death**, consistent with the letter never building toward a death per rule L14 — the fact panel carries his death, plainly, at the end.

**The exact wording of the greeting itself is deliberately left unquoted in the letter.** Quoting it exactly is a citation task for when this is fact-checked; the letter's job is the shape of the wait, not the content of the message.
