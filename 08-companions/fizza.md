## Letter — *Every Answer Was a Verse*

<!-- LETTER START -->
● In the house of Sayyida Fatima, there was a woman who worked there named Fizza. She helped run the whole household — the ordinary daily work of it, the kind that never really stops.

○ Did she belong to the family?

● Not by birth. She served in the house, which in that particular household meant she was close to everything that happened in it, every single day, in a way very few people outside the family itself ever were.

○ What was special about her?

● The way she is remembered is for how she talked.

○ How?

● Ask her anything, the story goes — a simple question, a hard one, it didn't matter — and for twenty years she answered only in lines from the Qur'an, never her own words, always a verse that happened to fit what was asked.

○ Twenty years? Every single answer?

● That is the story exactly as it is told, and it has been loved and retold for a very long time. We do not have it written down from when it happened, so we tell you plainly: this part is loved, not documented. Both can still be true.

○ How would that even work? What if someone asked what was for dinner?

● Nobody explains that part, which is exactly why the story has lasted this long — it is strange enough that people keep retelling it, generation after generation, trying to picture how she actually managed it day to day.

○ Why would she even do that?

● The accounts don't say why, only that she kept doing it, for that long, in that house, close to a family whose own speech people wrote down carefully and kept for a thousand years afterward, word by word.

○ Could you speak only in someone else's words? Try one sentence, out loud.

● However that just went for you, you'll have felt exactly how hard the discipline in this story actually is — not the cleverness of it. The discipline.

●○ Twenty years, and every single answer already written down somewhere else first.
<!-- LETTER END -->

## Fact panel — letter reverse

### FIZZA

● **Her place in the household.** Served in the house of Sayyida Fatima — not family by birth, but close to the daily life of it in a way few outside the family were.

● **What she is remembered for.** *Traditionally told:* for about twenty years, she is said to have answered every question put to her only in the words of the Qur'an, never her own.

● **What is not known.** How she managed it, or why she chose it. The accounts carry the fact of the practice, not an explanation for it.

● **Why it has lasted.** An unusual, specific, testable-sounding claim about someone otherwise undocumented — which is exactly the kind of detail that survives in memory even without a written record from the time.

● **Why she is in this envelope.** *Fizza served Sayyida Fatima's house, and is remembered for answering only in the words of the Qur'an.*

What is known of her life afterward is not well recorded.
