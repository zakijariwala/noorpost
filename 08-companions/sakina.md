## Letter — *Old Enough to Never Forget Him*

<!-- LETTER START -->
● Sakina was a small child, still young enough to need looking after, when she was at Karbala together with her father, Imam Husayn.

○ Was she old enough to actually understand what was happening around her?

● Old enough to be frightened by it, and old enough to love her father completely, and to remember him clearly for the rest of her whole life — which turned out, in the end, to be enough.

○ What happened to her afterward, once it was over?

● She survived, and was taken with the other captives on the long, hard march that followed — still a child, carrying nothing except what a child actually carries with them: memory, and grief, and a love that did not fade with time.

○ Is that remembered as something important, even though she didn't do anything?

● It is remembered, and carefully. Not a dramatic act, not a decision made under pressure like some of the others in this box — just a child's loyalty to her father, kept whole through everything that came after.

○ Why does that matter, if she didn't actually do anything herself?

● Because the story of that day usually gets told through decisions, and speeches, and who did what. Sakina's part of it is quieter than any of that, and just as real for being quiet.

○ Do you think a child's grief counts as much as a grown-up's does?

● It should. It rarely gets treated that way, which is exactly why this envelope exists — to say plainly that it does.

○ Is there someone you'd remember clearly, years later? Say who, out loud.

● Most people have somebody like that, if they actually stop to think about it. Sakina's whole part in this story is that kind of love, held onto, by a child.

●○ She was young enough to need looking after, and old enough to never once forget him.
<!-- LETTER END -->
