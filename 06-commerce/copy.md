# Storefront copy

The prose for the three shop pages. Editorial text belongs in markdown, not in a
Python string, so it can be edited without touching the generator.

`tools/build_shop.py` reads the `## key` headings below. Everything under a
heading is rendered as HTML in that slot. Adding a heading the generator does
not know about is harmless — it is ignored and reported at build time.

**Every claim here is about the product or the method.** None of it asserts a
fact about any of the Fourteen, because every such fact in this project is still
marked `TO VERIFY` and nothing prints on `TV`. No line is quoted from any source,
because permission has not been checked for a single one of the six fixed
editions. Both constraints are enforced by `tests/test_shop.py`, not by memory.

## landing.lede

Fourteen sealed envelopes, one for each of the Fourteen Masoomeen, opened by a
parent and a child together on the date it belongs to. About twenty-five minutes.

## landing.sub

The Islamic year has twelve months and the Fourteen do not divide evenly into it,
so two months carry two envelopes: Rajab and Sha'ban.

## landing.what-it-is

An envelope arrives sealed. Inside is a letter written in two voices — one for
the grown-up, one for the child — and the things that go with it. You read it out
loud, taking turns. There is a question to talk about, something to keep, and a
postcard to send back.

It is built to be re-read and kept, not skimmed once. The paper is warm ivory
because it is going to be handled.

## landing.first-edition

The first edition is two parts, and together they are a closed set.

**The Fourteen** is the monthly box — fourteen envelopes, one for each of the
Fourteen Masoomeen. **Everyone Else** is thirty-nine single envelopes, one per
person, dateless, bought one at a time. A second edition is where anyone new
would go.

## landing.envelope

Seven items in each envelope of the box.

## landing.envelope-note

The two mourning issues — Muharram and Safar — carry a pennant in place of the
sticker sheet, and one extra line inside the flap saying there is no game in this
one.

## landing.collections

Three collections build across the year: fourteen hadith cards sized for a
child's Qur'an, fourteen person prints for a wall, and fourteen event prints
punched for a ring. Forty-two pieces, three destinations, one box.

## landing.sourcing

**Shia sources only.** That is a hard rule in this project, and it has cost it
work — two well-known books were removed from the library after the rule was
written, and the envelope that had been fully checked against one of them went
back to unverified.

Every fact is traced to a named edition, a named translator and a number before
it prints. Where a saying is quoted it is quoted exactly, never trimmed to fit.
Where an account is loved but not documented from the time, the envelope says so
in the child's copy, not only the parent's. Where communities observe something
differently, the envelope declines to adjudicate and hands it to the parent.

## landing.proofs

These are typeset print proofs of one envelope, not finished artwork. No
illustrations have been drawn yet, and nothing here has been through a press.

## landing.availability

Nothing ships yet. All fourteen are printed before any of them go out, so that
monthly is a posting job and never a production job — and a subscription is not
sold before that has happened.

## about.what

Noor Post is a printed series for Shia families with children aged eight to
twelve, built around the Fourteen Masoomeen.

One envelope, opened together, on the date it belongs to. About twenty-five
minutes, of which the letter is four to four and a half.

## about.who

For a parent and a child, together. Not for a child alone, and not for a parent
to deliver at a child.

The letter is written in two voices and cannot be read by one person — the
grown-up's lines and the child's lines alternate, and the last line is read
together. Every activity in the envelope needs two people. Nothing in it needs
anything you do not already have, except a pen.

## about.method

**Two voices.** The grown-up reads one set of lines, the child reads the other.
The child's lines are set larger, in the display face, and no word in one should
stump an eight-year-old.

**One new thing for the adult, every envelope.** A fact a practising Shia adult
is unlikely to already know — a fact, not a reflection. It sits where the adult
will meet it while reading aloud. The test applied is to read it to three
practising adults; if two already knew it, it is replaced.

**No moral at the end.** The session card asks the question. The letter does not
answer it. What the child takes from it is theirs.

**The hardest part is left out.** The letter stops before it. Consequences may be
named; methods and final hours are not, and the death of the person the envelope
is about appears in exactly one place — the last line of the fact panel — and
nowhere else.

**One question runs through all fourteen.** What did he do with the freedom he
had?

## about.sourcing

Ethics and conduct only. Nothing jurisprudential, nothing theological, nothing
that would put a parent in the position of teaching a ruling. If a child asks a
ruling question, the envelope has done its job by prompting it, and the parent
takes it to their own family and their own scholar.

Published translations only, never an in-house rendering. One edition fixed per
work for the whole project, because page numbers move between editions. The
translator, the publisher and the year are named, because it is a credit and a
citation and both are owed.

The test every claim has to pass: could a hostile reader check this in an
afternoon and find that it holds? Anything that needs a caveat to survive is
marked as traditional or it is cut.

## about.refuses

**No depiction of any of the Fourteen.** No face, no figure, no likeness — shown
instead by setting, object or absence. A shield, a cloak, a doorway, an empty
road.

**No violence rendered in scene.**

**No adjudicating between communities.** Where they differ, one fixed line hands
the question to the parent, and it is never softened.

**No number that cannot be checked.** No follower counts, no population figures,
no claim about who was most anything.

**Nothing printed on an unverified row.** Every claim has a row on a citation
sheet with a status code, and a row that has not been checked against the fixed
edition does not reach print.

## about.status

Being honest about where this is: **nothing has been printed.** All fourteen
letters, fact panels and session cards are written, and thirty-nine companion
envelopes with them. No illustrations exist yet. No envelope has been printed,
timed with a real family, or reviewed. Every factual claim is still marked
unverified, and the scholar review that one envelope requires has not been
completed.

The build order is deliberate and it is not going to be shortcut: the rules
first, then one envelope, then a real conversation with a madrasa about what
forty sets would cost, then everything else.

## checkout.no-legal

**This form is not open yet.** Collecting an address needs a named data
controller and a privacy notice, and neither has been set up. The addresses would
be handled by a person, not a mailing platform, and nothing else would be sent.
