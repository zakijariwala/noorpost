## PAGE 2 — Where and when

> An oasis near Khaybar, in the Hijaz — known for its water and its date palms, valuable enough to be worth naming. In the later Medina years, it came to the Prophet not through battle but through a settlement, agreed rather than fought for, which is why it had a different status from land won by conquest.

---

## PAGE 3 — What happened, part one

> During the Prophet's lifetime, its yield was his to direct, and accounts describe it supporting his household and being given onward to travellers and to people in need.

---

## PAGE 4 — What happened, part two

> After the Prophet died, what should happen to Fadak became a question the community did not agree on. *Communities observe this differently. Ask your family which we follow.* This zine does not take a side in that disagreement — it stays with the land itself, not the argument over it.

---

## PAGE 5 — The thing most people do not know

> **The question did not stay settled for long, in either direction.**
>
> Long after everyone present at the original disagreement was gone, later rulers revisited Fadak's status more than once — at least one, generations afterward, is recorded restoring it to the Prophet's descendants. **A dispute from the first years after the Prophet kept resurfacing for centuries.** That alone says something about how much the land itself mattered, apart from who was right.

---

## PAGE 6 — What is there now

> Unlike Karbala or Najaf, Fadak has no single, agreed, marked site drawing visitors today. Its exact modern location is not settled the way the shrines are — a quieter kind of unresolved than the historical question, but the same shape.

---

## PAGE 7 — One thing to do

> **Ask your family about a disagreement over something owned — money, land, an object — that was never fully settled.**
>
> Not who was right. What happened to the relationship anyway, after.

## Production notes

**This is the one subject in the whole programme flagged as possibly not belonging in a free handout at all**, per `outlines.md`. It is drafted here on the strength of verbal scholar agreement communicated 2026-08-12 — that authorizes writing a draft to review, not printing one. **No line of this goes to production without the same written, line-by-line sign-off envelope 06 requires**, and for the same reason: this is the one other place in the product where the ground itself, not just the wording, is the risk.

**What this draft deliberately does not do:**
- Does not name who claimed what, or quote any position
- Does not describe the content of the disagreement, only that one existed and remains unresolved across communities
- Does not characterise the settlement that first brought the land to the Prophet as either generous or contested
- Applies the standard differ-line (`standard-lines.md` §1) at the one point where the disagreement itself has to be acknowledged at all, exactly as envelope 06 hands its own hardest ground to the parent rather than asserting anything

**Page 5 is the load-bearing page**, same rule as every other zine — and here it was chosen specifically because "the dispute resurfaced across centuries" is checkable, interesting to an eight-year-old, and requires no adjudication of the original disagreement to state. If the scholar review finds even this framing unsafe, the fix is a different page 5, not a softer version of this one — same instruction `outlines.md` gives for every zine's page 5.
