# Zines 03–15 — outlines

Page structure per `README.md`. Each entry fixes the cover, the *thing most people do not know* (page 5 — the reason the zine exists), what is there now (page 6), and the do-something (page 7).

**Twelve of these thirteen are now drafted to full text** — each subject below has its own file in `09-zines/` (e.g. `mubahala.md`) with the outline expanded to the 300–400 word, eight-page format. This file stays as the record of the load-bearing decision behind each one (the page 5 line) and as the source for Fadak, which is not drafted. **If a full draft and this outline ever disagree on the page 5 claim, the draft's own "Open" checklist governs — flag it, don't silently pick one.**

Page 5 is the load-bearing page in every one; if the page 5 line here does not survive checking, the zine needs a different subject rather than a softer page.

## 03 — Mubahala
**Release:** 24 Dhul Hijjah

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *An argument that did not happen* |
| 2 | Medina, 10 AH. A delegation of Christians from Najran comes to debate. |
| 3 | Days of argument that settle nothing. A different proposal is made: both sides bring their families and stand together. |
| 4 | The morning. Who was brought, and how few of them there were. |
| 5 | **It never took place.** The other side looked at who had been brought and declined, and a treaty was made instead. The famous event is an event that was called off. |
| 6 | Najran is in Saudi Arabia now, on the Yemeni border. The treaty terms are recorded. |
| 7 | Find an argument in this house that stopped because somebody was willing to stake something on it. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**Scholar:** the identification of who was brought. Handle with the differ line where it applies.

## 04 — Hudaybiyya
**Dateless**

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *The worst deal anybody ever signed* |
| 2 | A well outside Makkah, 6 AH. Fourteen hundred people arrive for pilgrimage, unarmed, and are stopped. |
| 3 | Negotiation. The terms are humiliating: go home, come back next year, and return anyone who comes to you. |
| 4 | The signing. Even the wording of the document is conceded — his own titles struck out at the other side's insistence. |
| 5 | **The two years after it were the biggest growth the community ever had.** The terms opened the roads. Being able to travel and talk turned out to be worth more than the concessions cost. |
| 6 | The site is on the Makkah boundary; a mosque marks it. |
| 7 | Name a time you agreed to something that felt like losing. What happened in the two years after? |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

## 05 — The Trench
**Dateless** · Ties to Salman, companions line

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *A hole, dug by hand, in time* |
| 2 | Medina, 5 AH. An army is coming that the city cannot fight in the open. |
| 3 | The idea comes from Salman, who is Persian, and it is a Persian technique nobody in Arabia has used: dig. |
| 4 | Weeks of digging by everybody, in cold, with not enough food. |
| 5 | **The attacking army arrived, saw the trench, and could not think of anything to do.** Cavalry against a ditch is nothing. They sat outside it for weeks and went home. **The battle is famous for a battle not happening.** |
| 6 | Medina has grown far past the line. The route is roughly known. |
| 7 | Measure a trench. Pace out five metres wide and stand at the edge. Then imagine a horse. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

## 06 — Jannat al-Baqi
**Dateless** · Ties to envelopes 02, 05, 09

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *The ground with nothing on it* |
| 2 | A burial ground beside the Prophet's mosque in Medina, in use since the first years of the city. |
| 3 | Who is there: four of the Fourteen, and a very large number of the first community. |
| 4 | What was there before 1925, and what happened to it. **Facts only, no adjectives.** |
| 5 | **It is the most visited unmarked ground in the world.** People come daily, in enormous numbers, and stand at a low wall looking at dirt, and know exactly who is where. |
| 6 | Photographs from before exist. Compare them with what visitors see now. |
| 7 | Look at a photograph of it. Then look at a photograph of Karbala. Ask why they look so different. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**Scholar, carefully.** Page 4 is the one page in the zine programme most likely to become a polemic. It carries dates and facts and stops.

## 08 — The road to Karbala
**Muharram**

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *Eight hundred miles* |
| 2 | Makkah to Kufa, the long way, in autumn. Who set out and roughly how many. |
| 3 | The stages, as a route: the stops, the days, the places where people joined and left. |
| 4 | Where it was blocked, and by whom, and how the destination changed. |
| 5 | **They were turned aside, not stopped.** Karbala was not where anybody was going. It is a place chosen by whoever was blocking the roads — an empty spot, off the route, with a river nearby and no town. |
| 6 | The route is walked now by very large numbers of people every Arbaeen. |
| 7 | Put the route on a map at home. Measure it against a journey you have done. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**Ends before Ashura.** Same rule as envelope 01.

| 2 | A garrison town in Iraq, founded new, on a grid, for soldiers. Not an old city. |

| 4 | What happened there afterwards, briefly and flatly. |

## 10 — Samarra
**Dateless** · Ties to envelopes 04, 11

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *A city built to hold an army* |
| 2 | Sixty miles up the Tigris from Baghdad. Built in a hurry in the 830s CE because Baghdad could not live with the caliph's soldiers. |
| 3 | What it was: barracks, palaces, a spiral minaret, straight roads, all new. |
| 4 | Two of the Fourteen were made to live there, and are buried there. |
| 5 | **It was abandoned within about sixty years.** An enormous purpose-built capital, put up fast and left. Most of it is still out there in the desert, and much of it has never been dug. |
| 6 | The archaeological site is one of the largest anywhere; the shrine is in the modern town. |
| 7 | Look at it from above on a map. The old grid is still visible from the air. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

## 11 — Fadak
**Dateless**

> **Scholar before drafting. Do not outline this one further without them.**

Fadak is a land dispute that is also a doctrinal dispute, and it sits directly on the ground envelope 06 was written to avoid. There is a version of this zine that is a careful account of a piece of agricultural land, its water, its yield and its documented history — and there is a version that is a polemic, and the second one writes itself if nobody is watching.

**Decide with the scholar whether this subject belongs in a free handout at all.** The other fourteen do not need it to carry the programme.

## 12 — Laylat al-Mabit
**1 Muharram**

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *The night somebody else slept in the bed* |
| 2 | Makkah, the night of the Hijra. The house is being watched by men who intend to act at dawn. |
| 3 | The plan: the Prophet leaves; Ali stays and sleeps in his place, in his cloak, so the house looks occupied. |
| 4 | Morning. They come in and find the wrong person. |
| 5 | **The plan only works if he lies still.** Not a fight, not a chase — hours of holding position in the dark knowing what is outside, doing nothing at all. **The hardest kind of courage to draw and the easiest to explain to a child.** |
| 6 | The house does not survive. The road out does. |
| 7 | Lie completely still for three minutes. Then multiply. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

## 13 — Dahw al-Ard
**25 Dhul Qa'dah**

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *The day the ground was spread out* |
| 2 | Not a place and not an event with witnesses — a date in the calendar about the earth itself. |
| 3 | What the day marks, and how it is observed. |
| 4 | Where it sits in the year, next to Dhul Hijjah. |
| 5 | **It is the only date in this whole calendar that is not about a person.** Fourteen envelopes, fourteen people; this day is about ground. |
| 6 | It is observed with fasting and specific prayers. *Communities observe this differently. Ask your family which we follow.* |
| 7 | Go outside and put your hand flat on actual ground. That is the entire observance for an eight-year-old. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**Careful:** this one drifts toward cosmology, which rule Q3 excludes. Keep it to the calendar, the observance and the ground.

## 14 — The Constitution of Medina
**Dateless** · Ties to envelope 03

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *An agreement, in writing, between people who did not like each other* |
| 2 | Medina, 1 AH. A town that had been in a long feud, plus arrivals with nothing. |
| 3 | What the document does: names the groups, sets out who defends whom, who pays what, who settles disputes. |
| 4 | Who signed: the tribes, the arrivals, and the Jewish clans of the town, named individually. |
| 5 | **It is administrative.** No sermon in it. It reads like a contract, because it is one, and it was written before there was any power to enforce it. |
| 6 | The text survives and is printed in translation. Anyone can read the whole thing in twenty minutes. |
| 7 | Write a one-page agreement for this house. Who does what, who decides, what happens when two people want the same thing. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**The best zine in the programme for a madrasa audience.** It is checkable, it is unusual, it is uncontested, and page 7 is a genuine activity.

## 15 — Bayt al-Hikma
**Dateless** · Ties to envelope 13

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | *A building full of translators* |
| 2 | Baghdad, in the decades around 800 CE. A library and translation project under the Abbasid caliphs. |
| 3 | What was done: Greek, Persian and Indian works carried into Arabic, by named people, on salaries. |
| 4 | Who worked there, named, including Christians and others who were not Muslims. |
| 5 | **Some of it came back.** Works that survived only in Arabic were later carried into Latin in Spain, which is how parts of Greek learning reached Europe at all. **The route runs Athens to Baghdad to Toledo.** |
| 6 | The building is gone. The manuscripts are in libraries around the world. |
| 7 | Find something in this house that was translated. Look for the translator's name. |
| 8 | Standard back cover. |

**Handle the superlatives.** "The greatest library in the world" is banned by rule F2. Named translators and named works do the job better.
