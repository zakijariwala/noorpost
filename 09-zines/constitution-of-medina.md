# Zine 14 — The Constitution of Medina

**Dateless** · Ties to envelope 03

---

## PAGE 1 — Cover

> # THE CONSTITUTION OF MEDINA
>
> *An agreement, in writing, between people who did not like each other*

Image: a rolled document, single ink. No figures.

---

## PAGE 2 — Where and when

> Medina, 1 AH. A town that had been through a long-running feud between its own tribes, now also home to new arrivals who own very little and need somewhere to belong.

---

## PAGE 3 — What the document does

> It sets out, in writing, who defends whom, who pays for what, and how disputes between groups get settled — naming the groups involved directly rather than speaking in generalities.

---

## PAGE 4 — Who signed

> The tribes of the city, the new arrivals, and the Jewish clans of Medina are all named individually as parties to it, each with stated obligations.

---

## PAGE 5 — The thing most people do not know

> **It is administrative.**
>
> No sermon in it anywhere. It reads like a contract, because that is exactly what it is — and it was written and agreed before the community that wrote it had any power to enforce a single line of it.

---

## PAGE 6 — What is there now

> The text survives and is available in translation. It is short enough that anyone curious can read the entire document start to finish in about twenty minutes.

---

## PAGE 7 — One thing to do

> **Write a one-page agreement for this house.**
>
> Who does what. Who decides when there's a disagreement. What happens when two people want the same thing at the same time. Keep it to one page — that's the discipline the original document kept too.

## Production notes

**The strongest zine in the programme for a madrasa audience**, per the outline — it is checkable against a surviving text, uncontested, and page 7 is a genuine activity rather than a reflection prompt dressed up as one. Lead with this one if only a few zines can be proofed and shown first.

**No paraphrase of specific clauses on page 3–4** without checking them against the actual surviving text — the appeal of this zine is that a reader can go check it themselves in twenty minutes, so nothing stated here should require softening once they do.
