---

## PAGE 2 — Where and when

> Medina, 5 AH. A large army is approaching that the city has no way to meet in open ground. Medina has walls on some sides and open approach on others — and it is the open side everyone is worried about.

---

## PAGE 3 — What happened, part one

> The idea that settles it comes from Salman, a Persian companion, and it is a Persian technique nobody in Arabia has used before: dig a trench along the exposed approach, wide enough that horses cannot cross it.

---

## PAGE 4 — What happened, part two

> Everyone digs — the Prophet included — for weeks, in cold weather, without much food. It is unglamorous, exhausting work, done by a whole city rather than a chosen few.

---

## PAGE 5 — The thing most people do not know

> **The attacking army arrived, looked at the trench, and could not think of anything to do about it.**
>
> Cavalry is not built to cross a ditch. There was no plan for one. They sat outside it for weeks, tried a few times, and eventually went home.
>
> **The battle is famous for a battle that mostly did not happen** — because one idea, borrowed from somewhere else and dug by hand, made the fight not worth having.

---

## PAGE 6 — What is there now

> Medina has grown far past where the trench line ran. The route it followed is roughly known and marked in the modern city, though little of the original earthwork remains to see.

---

## PAGE 7 — One thing to do

> **Measure a trench.**
>
> Pace out five metres on open ground and stand at the edge of it. Now picture a horse at a run, and picture trying to get it across that gap. That is the whole idea, and it is the whole reason it worked.
