# Envelope 03 — Rabi al-Awwal — Prophet Muhammad
## Letter, front: *The Cloak*

● is the grown-up. ○ is you. ●○ is together.

<!-- LETTER START -->

---

● Makkah was hot that year, and the Kaaba was broken. A flood had come down the valley and cracked the walls, so the families of the city pulled the old stones out to build it up again, higher than before.

○ Who was building it?

● Everyone. Every family took a wall. That was the point — nobody was going to let another family say they had built the House of God on their own. It went up fast.

● Then they came to the Black Stone.

○ What is the Black Stone?

● The stone set into the corner of the Kaaba. It is the oldest thing there, older than anyone could count back. It had to go into the new wall, and one man would carry it, and one family would be the family who did.

○ Everybody wanted to be them.

● Everybody wanted to be them. So for four days, nobody worked. Men sat in the shade with their arms folded and would not go near the wall. Then one family carried a bowl of blood into the courtyard and put their hands in it, which was how you said you would rather die than give way.

○ Over a stone?

● Over who they were.

○ Would you have given it up? Say it out loud before we go on.

● Nobody there did. They argued until an old man said: let the next person who walks through that gate settle it. Everyone agreed, because nobody knew who it would be.

● They looked up, and it was Muhammad, thirty-five years old. No army. No title. Nothing but a name the city had given him, and when they saw him they said it out loud before he had said anything at all.

○ What did they call him?

● Al-Amin. The one you can trust.

● He listened. Then he asked for a cloak, spread it flat on the ground, and lifted the stone into the middle with his own hands. Then he told each family to take an edge.

○ All of them at once?

● All of them at once. They carried it to the wall together, and he set it into its place himself. Nobody had won. Nobody had lost.

●○ Four days of arguing. One cloak.
<!-- LETTER END -->

### Notes on choices

**Why this beat.** Every child already knows the Prophet. Meeting him at thirty-five, before revelation, with the city already calling him al-Amin and having reasons, is the one new thing for the adult, and it arrives as a fact inside the story rather than as a claim about him.

**Why it stops where it stops.** No revelation, no Hira, no persecution. The envelope's event print carries the arrival at Quba and the fact panel carries the twenty-three years. The letter gets one afternoon.

**The line that demands a response** is deliberately unanswerable in the child's favour. They will say they would have given it up, and they will be wrong about themselves, and the session card's third question is built on that.

**"Would you rather die than give way"** was the hardest sentence in the draft. It names a custom without rendering it. Flag it for the scholar and for the family test — if an eight-year-old stalls here, the fix is to cut the bowl entirely and keep the four days.
