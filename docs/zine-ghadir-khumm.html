Ghadir Khumm

One sheet, folded to eight pages.

Page 1 · Cover

# GHADIR KHUMM

A place where a road forks

Page 2 · Where and when

It is not a city. It was a watering place — a pond, some trees, a stopping point on the road out of Makkah where the routes separate. Travellers going to Medina, to Egypt, to Iraq and to Yemen all part company here.

The year is 10 AH. The Prophet is coming back from Makkah with a very large number of people who have just done Hajj with him, and this is the last point at which they are all still together.

Page 3 · What happened, part one

He stopped the caravan. In the middle of the day, in that heat, at a place nobody stops longer than they have to.

The people at the front were called back. The people still coming were waited for. It took time — long enough that everyone understood something was going to be said, and that it had been timed for the one moment when the whole group was in one place.

They cleared ground and stacked the camel saddles so he could be seen.

Page 4 · What happened, part two

He spoke, and part of it was about what he was leaving behind and about being asked, one day, what people had done with it.

Then he took Ali by the hand and raised it, so that both of them could be seen by the whole crowd, and said the sentence this place is known for.

Communities observe this differently. Ask your family which we follow.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

It is the timing that is unusual, not the location.

A watering hole in the sun is a strange place to say anything important. Makkah would have been better. Medina would have been better.

But at Makkah, and at Medina, the crowd is a local crowd. At Ghadir the crowd is everybody, from everywhere, gathered once and about to scatter down four different roads to four different regions.

It is the last moment before the audience disperses, and it was chosen.

Page 6 · What is there now

Not much, and that is worth knowing before you go looking for photographs of it.

The pond is gone. The route has moved. What is marked today is an approximation, on a road most people drive past.

Karbala has a shrine. Najaf has a shrine. Ghadir has a fork in a road and a date in the calendar, and it has kept going for fourteen hundred years on that alone.

Page 7 · One thing to do

Find the last moment.

Ghadir works because of when it was, not where. The end of a trip. The last assembly. The point after which everyone goes home and does not all meet again.

Think of one group of people you are part of that will not be together forever. School class. Team. Cousins in one house at Eid.

What would you say, and when exactly would you say it?
