# BAYT AL-HIKMA

A building full of translators

Page 2 · Where and when

Baghdad, in the decades around 800 CE. A library and translation project operating under the patronage of the Abbasid caliphs, at a court where Imam al-Rida's own generation lived.

Page 3 · What was done there

Works in Greek, Persian and Indian languages were carried into Arabic, by named scholars, working for pay rather than as an incidental hobby.

Page 4 · Who worked there

Named individuals worked on this project, including Christians and others who were not Muslim — a working group defined by the skill of translation, not by a single faith.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

Some of it came back the other way.

Certain works of Greek learning had already been lost in their original language by this point and survived only because they had been carried into Arabic. Centuries later, some of those Arabic versions were carried again — into Latin, in Spain — which is part of how fragments of Greek learning reached Europe at all. The route runs Athens to Baghdad to Toledo.

Page 6 · What is there now

The building itself no longer stands. The manuscripts that survive from this era are held in libraries around the world today, some far from where they were written or translated.

Page 7 · One thing to do

Find something in this house that was translated from another language.

A book, packaging, a label — anything. Look for the translator's name. Most people never notice it is there.
