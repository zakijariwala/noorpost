Thirty-Four, Thirty-Three, Thirty-Three

● the grown-up  ·  ○ the child  ·  ●○ together

●The work in that house was not light work. Water came from a well and had to be carried. Flour did not exist until somebody made it, and making it meant a stone wheel turned by hand, for as long as it took, most days.

○Every day?

●Most days. Bread was not a thing you bought in that city. It was a thing somebody in the house made, starting from grain, before anybody ate. Her hands showed it. And there had been a change in the city that year — there were more people now, and other households had somebody to help with the heavy work.

●So she went to ask.

○Ask for what?

●For someone to help with the house. That was the request. Not a small strange spiritual request. She had turned that wheel until her hands were marked and she asked her father for help.

○Did she get it?

●Not what she asked for. She went home without it, and the answer came to her afterwards, and it was not a person. It was something to say.

○That's it?

●That is it. Thirty-four times Allahu akbar. Thirty-three times alhamdulillah. Thirty-three times subhanallah. A hundred, on the fingers, taking about a minute.

○How is that better than help?

●I don't know. That is the honest answer and you should have it. Something was asked for, something else was given, and the thing given is still being used in this house fourteen hundred years later, which is more than can be said for any servant anyone hired that year.

○Would you have been disappointed? Say it out loud before we go on.

●She used it every night. Her sons learned it from her. It has a name now — people call it her tasbih, and they say it after prayers, and there are people saying it right now, in the dark, in cities she never heard of.

●You can do it tonight. It takes one minute. You will not need anything you do not already have.

●○Thirty-four. Thirty-three. Thirty-three.

Letter, reverse

SAYYIDA FATIMA AL-ZAHRA

(peace be upon her) b. Makkah · d. 11 AH, Medina

●Where and when. Born in Makkah before the Hijra, and lived the second half of her short life in Medina. Communities differ on the year of her birth.

●Who was ruling. In Makkah, the clan assembly and no king. In Medina, her father, in a city that had invited him to settle its arguments and then kept him to run them.

●Her lifetime. Short. She outlived her father by a matter of months, and she was younger when she died than most of the people who now read about her.

●What she left. The tasbih that carries her name, said after prayer by people who have never read a word about her otherwise. Her sons. And a sermon she gave in the masjid, in front of the city, which was written down and is still read.

●Fizza. She was in that household, and she was not from Arabia — accounts say she came from far away and had been a servant. Traditionally told: for about twenty years she answered every question put to her in verses of the Qur'an, and did not use her own words.

●Elsewhere in the world. At about the same time, in England, a king was buried in a ship under a mound of earth at a place now called Sutton Hoo, with his helmet and his gold around him.

Session card — Conversation

Talk about it

About ten minutes. No right answers, and no points.

1. She asked for help and got something to say instead. Has that ever happened to you?

2. ● Grown-up answers this one first. Tell about a time you asked for help and did not get it. What did you do instead?

3. The work in that house was carrying water and grinding flour. What is the equivalent job here, and who actually does it?

4. A hundred words takes one minute. What else in your day takes one minute, and do you notice it?

5. ⚑ This one is hard for a grown-up. She asked her own father for help with the house and he did not give it. Would you have?

Last: Say it together tonight, once, before bed. Thirty-four, thirty-three, thirty-three.
