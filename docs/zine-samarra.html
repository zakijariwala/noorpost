# SAMARRA

A city built to hold an army

Page 2 · Where and when

On the Tigris, north of Baghdad. Built quickly in the 830s CE, because Baghdad could no longer comfortably hold the caliph's soldiers alongside everyone else living there.

Page 3 · What happened, part one

What went up was barracks, palaces, a famous spiral minaret, and long straight roads — an entire new capital, built from nothing in a short span of years.

Page 4 · What happened, part two

Two of the Fourteen were made to live there for years, watched closely by the state, and are buried there still.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

It was abandoned within about sixty years.

An enormous, purpose-built capital — palaces, barracks, avenues — put up fast and then walked away from once the reasons for it had passed. Most of it is still out there in the desert, and large parts of it have never been excavated.

Page 6 · What is there now

The archaeological site is one of the largest of its kind anywhere, largely open ground today. The shrine associated with the Fourteen sits within the modern town that has grown up around one corner of the old capital.

Page 7 · One thing to look at

Look at Samarra from above, on a map.

The old grid — the straight roads laid out in the 830s — is still visible from the air, faint lines across empty ground, even though almost nothing was built to last.
