The Man Who Watched All Night

● the grown-up  ·  ○ the child  ·  ●○ together

●Tawus was a scholar, respected in his own right, the kind of person other people came to with their questions rather than the other way around.

○What was he doing near the Kaaba at night?

●Watching, quietly, from a respectful distance that didn't intrude on anything. There was a man who came, alone, most nights, long after almost everyone else in the city had gone to sleep, and prayed for hours until the sky itself slowly changed color.

○Who was the man?

●Imam al-Sajjad. Nobody had sent him there, and nobody was checking whether he came or not, or how long he stayed each time. He simply came back, night after night, for years on end, and nobody but a small handful of people like Tawus ever saw any of it happen.

○Did he know Tawus was watching?

●Probably not, or he simply didn't mind either way. That's part of what struck Tawus so deeply about the whole thing — nothing about the practice ever changed depending on who might happen to be looking that particular night.

○What did Tawus do about it, afterward?

●He kept watching, quietly, from that same respectful distance, and he kept the memory carefully enough that it actually survived him — passed on, retold with care by people who understood what they were carrying, until it eventually reached us.

○Isn't that strange, just watching someone pray, over and over?

●It is strange, until you notice what it actually proves: this was never a performance for an audience. There was no audience to perform for, and no reason to expect one, ever, on any given night. That's the entire reason it's still worth remembering today, all these years later.

○Would you do it if nobody would ever know? Say one thing out loud.

●Most people can imagine doing it once. Doing it for years, completely unwitnessed, is a very different matter entirely.

●○Nobody sent him, nobody checked, and he went anyway, every single night.

Letter, reverse

TAWUS AL-YAMANI

●Who he was. A respected early scholar from Yemen, known in his own right for learning, not primarily as anyone's attendant.

●What he witnessed. Imam al-Sajjad's private night prayer near the Kaaba — long, repeated, unrequested, and largely unseen by the wider community.

●Why it is remembered. Because it was unwitnessed by design. Nothing about the practice changed whether or not anyone was present to see it.

●What he did with it. Kept the account and passed it on carefully enough that it is still told today.

●Why he is in this envelope. Tawus watched Imam al-Sajjad pray, alone, all night, for years, without ever being asked to.

Little else of his life connects directly to this account.
