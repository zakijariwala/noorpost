Page 1 · Cover

# JANNAT AL-BAQI

The ground with nothing on it

Page 2 · Where and when

A burial ground beside the Prophet's mosque in Medina, in use since the earliest years of the city — the oldest Islamic cemetery associated with the Prophet's own lifetime.

Page 3 · What happened, part one

Four of the Fourteen are buried here, along with a very large number of the first generation of the community — companions, family, people who knew the Prophet directly.

Page 4 · What is there now, fact only

Structures and domes marking these graves stood for centuries. In 1925 they were demolished, and the site has stood as open, unmarked ground since. This is a documented event with a documented date; it is stated here without further comment.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

It is the most visited unmarked ground anywhere.

Visitors come daily, in large numbers, and stand at a low wall looking at open dirt with nothing built on it — and know exactly who is where, because the memory of the place has outlasted anything that was ever built on it.

Page 6 · Comparing then and now

Photographs from before 1925 exist and can be found in print and online. Set beside a photograph of the site today, they show plainly what changed and what did not.

Page 7 · One thing to look at

Look at a photograph of Jannat al-Baqi. Then look at a photograph of Karbala.

Ask why they look so different today. Don't answer it for them — the question is the activity.
