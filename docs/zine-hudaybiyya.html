Page 1 · Cover

# HUDAYBIYYA

The worst deal anybody ever signed

Page 2 · Where and when

A well outside Makkah, 6 AH. Fourteen hundred people set out from Medina for pilgrimage — unarmed, in pilgrim dress, not intending to fight anyone — and are stopped short of the city.

Page 3 · What happened, part one

Negotiation follows instead of a fight. The terms that come out of it are hard to accept: turn back this year, come again next year, and anyone who leaves Makkah to join the Prophet's side is to be sent back — while the same is not promised in return.

Page 4 · What happened, part two

The document is written up, and even the writing of it is conceded — the wording the Prophet asks for is objected to by the other side, and he has it changed rather than let the agreement fail over words.

Many of his own companions think it is a defeat. Some say so to his face.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

The two years after the treaty were the biggest growth the community ever saw.

A truce, even a humiliating one, opens the roads. People who would never have travelled into a warzone could now travel to talk, to see, to decide for themselves. Being able to move and talk turned out to be worth far more than everything conceded to get it.

Page 6 · What is there now

The site sits on the boundary of the modern Makkah area, and a mosque marks it. The well the place is named for is still referenced in accounts of the spot.

Page 7 · One thing to do

Name a time you agreed to something that felt like losing.

Not to feel better about it — to check. What actually happened in the two years after? Sometimes a bad deal is just a bad deal. Sometimes it is the thing that opened everything else up.
