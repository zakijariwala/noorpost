The Better Shirt

● the grown-up  ·  ○ the child  ·  ●○ together

●Two men went to a market in Kufa to buy clothes. One of them was running an empire that stretched from Egypt to the edge of India. The other one worked for him, in his house, and had been bought and freed by him years before.

○Which one had the money?

●Both, that day. They were buying two shirts, one each, and the shirts were not the same. One was a bit better. Slightly nicer cloth, a bit more money.

○Who got it?

●Have a guess first. Actually — don't guess. Just say what you think should happen and see whether you were right.

○The one in charge?

●Qambar got the better one. The man running the empire took the cheaper shirt and wore it, and there is a reason recorded for it, and the reason is not that he was being humble at anybody.

○What was it?

●That Qambar was younger. Young men like nice clothes and it matters to them more, and he said so, and that was the end of it.

○That's the whole reason?

●That is the whole reason. Not a lesson, not a demonstration, not something done in front of an audience to make a point. Two men bought two shirts and the younger one got the better shirt because he would enjoy it more.

○Would you have taken it? Say yes or no honestly.

●Now think about who this man was. He appointed the judges. He commanded the army. Every governor in that empire held their job at his word. And in a market in his own capital he is haggling over which of two shirts he takes home, and losing.

●Qambar stayed with him for the rest of his life. He is in the accounts in the ordinary way — carrying things, answering the door, being sent to fetch someone. He is not there because he did something enormous once. He is there because he was there every day.

●○He got the better shirt. That is the story.

Letter, reverse

QAMBAR

●Who he was. A member of Imam Ali's household in Kufa. He had been enslaved, and was bought and freed by him, and stayed on afterwards in his service.

●How he appears in the record. Constantly, and in small ways — carrying messages, at the door, sent to bring somebody. He is one of the very few people in these accounts we see doing ordinary daily work.

●The shirts. The account of the two shirts is reported with the reason given: that the younger man would take more pleasure in the better one.

●What it tells you. The household of a man running an empire had almost nothing in it. The reports agree on this from several directions — plain food, plain clothes, a house you would not pick out.

●Afterwards. He remained loyal to Imam Ali's memory under a government that punished exactly that, and the accounts say it cost him his life under al-Hajjaj.

●Why he is in this envelope. Qambar served Imam Ali.

He was killed at Kufa, on the orders of al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf.
