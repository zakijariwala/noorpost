Dahw al-Ard

One sheet, folded to eight pages.

Page 1 · Cover

# DAHW AL-ARD

The day the ground was spread out

Page 2 · Where and when

Not a place, and not an event with witnesses to describe — a date on the calendar, marked every year, about the earth itself rather than about a person.

Page 3 · What the day marks

This is the one date in the whole calendar that is not tied to something a person did. It marks the earth being spread out and made ready — the day is about ground, not about a life.

Page 4 · Where it sits in the year

It falls on 25 Dhul Qa'dah, a few weeks ahead of Dhul Hijjah and the Hajj season that follows it.

Page 5 · The thing most people do not know

It is the only date in this entire calendar that is not about a person.

Fourteen envelopes, fourteen people, one date a year for each of them — and then this one, sitting outside that pattern entirely, about ground instead of about anyone in particular.

Page 6 · How it is observed

Communities mark it with fasting and specific prayers. Communities observe this differently. Ask your family which we follow.

Page 7 · One thing to do

Go outside and put your hand flat on actual ground.

That is the entire observance, for an eight-year-old, on a day that is about the ground and nothing more complicated than that.

Page 8 · Back cover

Sources on the back.
