Envelope 01 · Muharram

The Man Who Was Sent to Stop Them

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●The army came out of the heat with a thousand horses and nothing left in their water skins. Their commander was a man called Hurr, and his orders were simple. Find Husayn on the road. Do not let him go on, and do not let him turn back.

○Did he stop them?

●He did. He put his thousand men across the road. And then, before he said one word about the orders, he asked for water.

○For his own army?

●For his own army. And Husayn gave it. All of it. His people watered a thousand horses one at a time, tipping the bowls slowly so the animals would not drink too fast and hurt themselves.

○They were there to trap him.

●They were. He watered them anyway.

●Hurr rode alongside them for days after that, closing off every road that turned for home. He was good at his job. When they stopped at Karbala, it was Hurr's men who picked the ground.

○So he was on the wrong side.

●He was on the side he had been sent to be on. There is a difference, and it did not help him sleep.

○Would you have given them the water? Say yes or no now.

●On the last morning the two armies stood facing each other in the cold, and Hurr sat on his horse and shook. A man next to him said he had never once seen him frightened. Hurr said he was not frightened. He said he was choosing, and that he had never had to choose before.

●Then he turned his horse and rode out alone, across the flat empty ground between the two armies, and got down in front of Husayn and asked him a question. He asked whether a man like him — the man who brought the army, who picked the ground — could still be forgiven this late in the day.

○What did he say?

●He said yes. He said it straight away.

●Hurr was given his name on the day he was born. It means the free one. He carried it for a lifetime and used it once, for about ten minutes.

●○Free. That was his name the whole time.
