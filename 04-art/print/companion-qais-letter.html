Everyone Else · Qais ibn Sa'd

The General Who Obeyed Anyway

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●Qais commanded part of Imam Hasan's army — a real soldier's job, trusted with real soldiers, in a war that genuinely mattered to how things would go afterward.

○Was he good at it?

●Very good. The kind of commander soldiers actually trust with their lives, out on the field, which is rarer than it sounds and takes far longer to earn than it ever takes to lose.

○So what happened?

●Imam Hasan decided to end the war with a treaty instead of a battle. Peace, on hard terms that cost a great deal to accept, instead of a fight Qais genuinely believed could still be won outright.

○Did Qais agree with that?

●No. He thought it was plainly the wrong call, and unlike a lot of people in his exact position, he did not quietly pretend otherwise to himself just to make the day easier.

○Did he say so?

●He said so, plainly, to the Imam's face, before anything was final. That part matters as much as what came after — he did not obey silently while secretly resenting it for years.

○Then what did he actually do, once the decision was finally made?

●He obeyed anyway. Laid down the command he disagreed with, and carried out the terms exactly as if he'd agreed with every single word of them from the very start.

○Why would you do what you think is wrong?

●Because he trusted the person deciding more than he trusted his own read of that particular moment. That is a harder thing to do than winning a battle, and from the outside it doesn't look like anything at all.

○Could you actually do that? Name a time somebody asked you to, out loud.

●Most people can name the time they were asked. Far fewer can honestly name a time they actually went through with it, all the way, without complaining afterward to anyone who'd listen.

●○He thought it was wrong, and he did it exactly right anyway.
