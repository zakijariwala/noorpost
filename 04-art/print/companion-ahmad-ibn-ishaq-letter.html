Everyone Else · Ahmad ibn Ishaq al-Qummi

What He Was Shown

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●Ahmad's whole city trusted him with its questions — the real, ongoing job of carrying them to Imam al-Askari, and bringing honest answers all the way back home again.

○That sounds like a very big responsibility to carry.

●It was, and it was never a one-time errand — he made that same long trip repeatedly, over years, which is its own particular kind of trust, renewed again each time he set out.

○Did people just choose him once, or did he actually earn it?

●Both, really. Nobody hands a job like that to someone once and then simply stops checking. He kept it because he kept being right for it, trip after trip, year after year.

○What happened on one of those particular trips?

●Imam al-Askari showed him something very few people outside his own household had ever been allowed to see.

○What was it, exactly?

●A child — his own son, still quite young, kept quietly out of general view because of how much real danger surrounded that entire household at the time.

○Why show it to Ahmad specifically, out of everyone?

●Because the Imam trusted him enough, after all those years, to let him carry that back too — not just questions and answers this time, but something the whole wider community badly needed real reassurance of.

○Did Ahmad fully understand what he was being shown, right then?

●Not fully, probably, not at the exact time it happened. Trust of that kind is usually only understood afterward, sometimes much later, once you look back and see what it actually meant.

○Has anyone trusted you before you understood why? Say what it was, out loud.

●Most people can point to at least one thing exactly like that, though usually only years later, looking back.

●○He carried the city's questions for years, and once, he carried something no question could have prepared him for.
