Envelope 10 · Sha'ban

Letters In, Answers Out

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●For about seventy years there was an address.

○An address?

●Not a house you could visit. A man. You went to him with your question written down, or with money you owed, or with something you needed settled, and you handed it over. And some time later, an answer came back.

○From who?

●That is exactly what everybody wanted to know, and the answer was: you do not get to know, and you do not get to go, and the man in front of you is not going to tell you.

○Did people trust that?

●Enough of them did. Money went in — real money, from real cities, from people who had worked for it. Questions went in. Answers came out, in writing, and people who knew the handwriting said it was the same hand for a very long time.

●There were four of them, one after another. Not four at once. When the first one was dying, he named the second. When the second was dying, he named the third. And so on. Each one was known by name, in public, and each one did the job for years.

○How long altogether?

●About seventy years. Long enough that a person could be born, live a whole life as a merchant or a teacher, and die, with this being simply how things were.

○And then?

●The fourth one received a letter, and the letter said there would be no fifth. It said not to accept anyone who claimed the job after him. And that was the end of the arrangement.

○Would you have written to him? Say what you would have asked.

●Nobody has an address now. There is no one to hand a letter to, and there has not been for more than a thousand years, and that is not a gap in the story. That is the story.

●Every other envelope in this box has an ending in it. Somebody is born, somebody lives, and there is a last line. This one does not have one, because it has not happened yet.

●○We are inside this part.
