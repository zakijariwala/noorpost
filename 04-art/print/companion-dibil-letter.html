The Poem He Recited Anyway

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●Dibil was a poet — the kind of poet people invited into a room specifically to hear what he had written, which is its own particular sort of danger.

○Why would poetry be dangerous?

●Because a poem is a public thing, said out loud, in front of real witnesses. Once it's said, it can't be quietly taken back afterward, and everyone left in the room remembers exactly who said it, for a very long time.

○What did he write, this time?

●A long poem about the family of the Prophet — their history, and what had been done to them over the years, told plainly, without softening a single line of it for anyone's comfort.

○Was that sort of poem allowed?

●Not comfortably, no. It was the kind of poem most people composing it would keep strictly for private company, not recite anywhere it might be overheard, remembered, and repeated afterward.

○Did he recite it anyway?

●He did — in front of Imam al-Rida himself, at a time and in a place where the wrong lines, said in front of the wrong audience, could genuinely have cost him a very great deal indeed, and everyone in that room understood it.

○Was the Imam upset by it?

●The opposite, entirely. He listened all the way through without interrupting once, and when it finally ended, he gave Dibil his own cloak as a gift — a real, genuinely valuable thing, offered right there on the spot in front of everyone.

○Just for reciting a poem?

●For the poem, and for having said the whole of it, in front of him, without flinching once partway through and stopping.

○Would you say something true if it made others uncomfortable? Say it out loud.

●Most people already know the difference between believing something quietly and actually saying it where it counts.

●○He said the whole poem, start to finish, and the Imam gave him his own cloak.
