Envelope 09 · Sha'ban

The List

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●After Karbala there was one grown man left in that family, and he was ill on the day it happened, which is the only reason he was not out on that field. He was taken as a prisoner to Kufa, and then to Damascus, and then eventually he was let go, and he went home to Medina.

○And then what?

●And then, for about thirty-four years, not much anyone could put in a history book. He did not raise an army. He did not make speeches in the market. He mostly prayed, and people who wrote down what he said in his prayers ended up with a book.

○That's it?

●No. He also did one thing nobody had done in quite that way. He sat down and made a list.

○A list of what?

●Of every single person and thing you owe something to. Not what you are owed. What you owe. He went through it one by one, and said what each debt was.

●Your tongue is on the list. He says you owe it the duty of keeping it away from what is foul.

○You can owe your tongue something?

●And your eyes. And your ears, and your hands, and your feet. Your neighbour is on there. Your teacher is on there. The person you are teaching is on there, separately, with different things owed.

●Your mother. Your father. Your child — a whole entry about what a parent owes a child, which is not the entry a child expects to find.

○Is there one for someone who is horrible to you?

●There is. There is an entry for the person who has wronged you, and it says what you owe them, which is not nothing, and it is the hardest one on the list.

○Which of these do you owe me? Ask before we go on.

●And here is what makes it a list and not a sermon. It does not say be good. It says: here is who, here is what, go and do it. Fifty-odd entries. You could read the whole thing tonight.

●Nobody made him write it. He had every reason to be finished with the world.

●○Everybody you owe. Written down.
