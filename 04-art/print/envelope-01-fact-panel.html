IMAM HUSAYN

(peace be upon him) b. 4 AH, Medina · d. 61 AH, Karbala

●Where and when. He lived in Medina, in the city his grandfather built the community in, and died about eight hundred miles away on open ground in Iraq that had no town on it and no name most people knew.

●Who was ruling. The Umayyad caliph Yazid ibn Muawiya, from Damascus. The order that reached Karbala came through his governor at Kufa, Ubaydullah ibn Ziyad. Both names are worth saying out loud once — the caliph never came.

●The imamate. About ten years, from 50 AH, from the age of about forty-six. He was an old man by the standards of his time when he set out.

●What he left. Names. We know who was with him, one by one, and the lists survive — his brothers, his sons, his companions, and the men who crossed over on the last morning. Nothing else that happened that year in that empire is recorded person by person.

●Umm al-Banin. She was married to Imam Ali and had four sons, and all four were at Karbala, and none came back. In the accounts of what she asked afterwards, she asked after Husayn first. Traditionally told.

●Elsewhere in the world. In the same years, bishops were gathering at Constantinople for a council that ran into 681 CE, arguing over a question of doctrine that had split the empire for fifty years.

He was killed at Karbala in 61 AH, with his family and companions, by the army sent against him by Yazid ibn Muawiya.
