Everyone Else · Halima al-Sa'diyya

The Desert Mother

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●It was the custom in Makkah at that time to send newborn babies out to families living in the desert, away from the city, to be nursed and raised there for their first few years.

○Why send a baby away from home like that?

●The desert air was thought to be healthier for a small child, and the language purer, away from a crowded, busy city. Whole families made a modest living taking in babies exactly this way, every season.

○Did families choose which baby to take?

●They did, every year, picking whichever babies seemed likely to bring the best payment or the best connections — an ordinary, practical arrangement for everyone involved.

○Did anyone want to take the Prophet in?

●At first, not really, no. He had no father still living, which usually meant no payment worth the trouble to most families, and the ones choosing that particular year passed him over for babies with wealthier parents instead.

○Who took him in, then, in the end?

●Halima, whose own family had almost nothing that year — a weak, thin camel, barely any milk to speak of, a hard season all around. She took him mostly because nobody else had, not because she expected very much from it.

○Did anything actually change after that?

●Everything, by her own later account of it. The camel gave milk again, the land itself seemed to give more, and the family's whole fortune quietly turned around during the exact years he stayed with them.

○Would you take on something nobody else wanted? Say why, out loud.

●Halima didn't know any of what was coming, not one part of it. She simply took in the baby nobody else in that whole group would.

●○The family with almost nothing took the child nobody else wanted, and never once called that their hardest year again.
