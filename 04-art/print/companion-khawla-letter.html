Everyone Else · Khawla bint al-Azwar

The Rider Nobody Recognized

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●During a battle in the years after the Prophet, word reached the camp that Khawla's brother had been captured, taken by the opposing side, and was being held away from the main fighting.

○What did she do when she heard that?

●She armed herself fully, covered her face the way any soldier riding out that day might, and went out into the battle alone, without waiting for anyone's permission first.

○Was that allowed, a woman riding out like that?

●It was not the ordinary custom, no — which is exactly why nobody riding past her that morning had any reason to look twice.

○Did nobody recognize her riding past them?

●Nobody at all. She rode and fought exactly as though she were simply another soldier among many, and pushed steadily toward where the captives were being held that day.

○Did she actually reach her brother in the end?

●She did, and freed him there, still without anyone in either army realizing that the rider who had done it was a woman who had ridden out from her own tent that same morning.

○Did people find out afterward who it had really been?

●Eventually, yes — and it became one of the stories told about that day, though accounts of exactly what happened vary a good deal depending on who is telling it.

○Would you ride into danger for someone? Say what would stop you, out loud.

●Most people never actually have to find out the answer to that. Khawla rode out before she could talk herself into turning back.

●○Nobody recognized the rider, and the rider never once stopped to be recognized.
