Envelope 07 · Rajab · Fact panel

IMAM ALI IBN ABI TALIB

(peace be upon him) b. 13 Rajab, about 30 years before the Hijra, Makkah · d. 40 AH, Kufa

●Where and when. Makkah, then Medina, then Kufa — a garrison town in Iraq that he made his capital because it was where the people were, not because it was comfortable.

●Who was ruling. Him. This is the only envelope in the box where the family is the government, and that is what makes the letter possible. Everything he could have done, he could actually have done.

●The imamate and the caliphate. The imamate about thirty years, from 11 AH. The caliphate about four and a half years, from 35 AH. Note the gap between those two numbers. For twenty-four years he was not in charge of anything.

●What he left. Letters and sermons, collected about three hundred and fifty years later into the book called Nahj al-Balagha. His instructions to Malik al-Ashtar on how to govern Egypt are in it — a long letter about how to treat people you have power over, written by someone who had it.

●Fatima bint Asad. His mother. She raised the Prophet in her own house after his grandfather died, and the accounts hold that she gave birth to Ali inside the Kaaba itself.

●Elsewhere in the world. In the same years, across the world in the forests of Central America, the Maya were cutting dates into stone at cities like Tikal — a different empire, a different calendar, and also writing down who ruled and when.

He was killed in the masjid at Kufa in 40 AH, during the dawn prayer, by Abd al-Rahman ibn Muljam.
