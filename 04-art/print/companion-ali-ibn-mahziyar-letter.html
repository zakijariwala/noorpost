Everyone Else · Ali ibn Mahziyar

The Shipment That Always Arrived

● the grown-up  ·  ○ is you  ·  ●○ together

●Ali worked by sea — a merchant with his own ships, moving cargo along routes that could take many weeks and went wrong often enough that everyone simply accepted the risk as normal.

○What did he actually carry for Imam al-Jawad?

●Funds, letters, ordinary goods — the everyday substance of a household trying to stay in touch with people it could not simply go and visit, moved the same unremarkable way any working merchant moved anything at all.

○Was that dangerous work, doing it that way?

●Sea routes always carried some real danger — storms, long delays, cargo that simply didn't arrive at the other end at all. It happened to other merchants regularly enough that nobody ever thought any less of them for it.

○But not to Ali, apparently?

●Not to Ali, apparently, no. Year after year, shipment after shipment, whatever he carried simply arrived, safely, on time. Nothing lost at sea, nothing late enough to matter, nothing ever quietly unaccounted for.

○Isn't that just good luck, though?

●Maybe some small part of it genuinely was luck. But luck alone doesn't explain someone being trusted with the very same kind of cargo, again and again, year after year, for years on end — that part is earned honestly, one safe delivery at a time, the slow and unglamorous way.

○How do you actually earn that kind of trust?

●There is only one way there really is: by being right, reliably, for long enough that people simply stop needing to check up on you at all.

○Name something people trust you to get right, out loud.

●Most people can name something, if they think about it for a moment. Fewer can honestly say they've never once let it slip, even a little.

●○He carried it, every single time, and it was there exactly when it was meant to be.
