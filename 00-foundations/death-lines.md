# The fourteen death lines

Drafted together, in one sitting, on purpose. Written one at a time across a year they will drift in tone, in how much they name, and in how much they spare. Drift is what a parent notices when they have read six of them.

## The rule

**The death is the last line of the fact panel and appears nowhere else in the envelope.**

Name the fact and the actor. Drop the method. No symptoms. No final hours. No scene.

## The shape

> He died in [place] in [year] AH. [Actor clause.]

The actor clause names who is held responsible in the traditional Shia account, and stops. It does not say how.

**"Poisoned" is a method, not a fact.** Eight of these end in poison in the traditional accounts and the word appears in none of them. `was killed on the orders of X` carries the whole fact a child needs and none of the picture they do not.

## Status of this file

Draft wordings. **Years and actors are marked for verification** — every one must be checked against the citation sheet before Gate 3, and the whole file goes to the named scholar as a single document rather than envelope by envelope. Reviewing them together is the only way to catch inconsistency in what is conceded and what is asserted.

## Three problems this file surfaced

### 1. Envelope 14 — Umm al-Fadl is in two slots at once

The PRD assigns **Umm al-Fadl, the caliph's daughter whom he married**, to the woman slot in envelope 14. Traditional accounts of Imam al-Jawad's death implicate her directly.

So the fact panel would carry her as its woman and then, four lines later, name a death she is traditionally held party to. A parent who knows the account will read that as either careless or as an accusation the panel is making sideways.

Three ways out:

1. **Keep her, drop the actor to al-Mu'tasim only.** The draft above does this. The panel never connects her to the death, and the account stays available to any parent who knows it.
2. **Replace her.** Sayyida Hakima Khatun, his aunt, is already in envelope 10 and would repeat. His mother Sabika is thinly documented. Neither is clean.
3. **Name the tension in the parent's copy.** Costs a line and puts an accusation in an envelope aimed at eight-year-olds.

**Recommendation: option 1, and flag it to the scholar explicitly.** Do not let this one pass as a detail — it is exactly the kind of thing that reads as an editorial position once it is printed.

### 2. Zaynab appears twice

The women list runs Umm al-Banin · Zaynab · Khadija · Nargis · **Zaynab** · Fizza · … Fourteen slots, thirteen distinct women. Envelope 02 gives her Arbaeen; envelope 05 gives her the Damascus shrine and her birth date.

Defensible, since the two envelopes use her differently, but it is the one repeat in a feature whose whole selling point is fourteen. Decide deliberately before Gate 3. Candidates for envelope 05 if she moves: Umm Salama, who is documented and connects to Karbala through the account of the soil.

### 3. Envelope 03 date

The Prophet's death is 28 Safar in the Shia calendar and 12 Rabi al-Awwal in most Sunni accounts — the same dispute as his birth. Both dates take the standard differ-by-community line from `standard-lines.md`.

## Envelope 06 — blocking

**Do not draft this line. The named scholar writes it.**

The circumstances of Sayyida Fatima's death sit at the centre of the Sunni–Shia dispute and readers will expect it told one way. The letter avoids the ground entirely — it is about the tasbih — and the fact panel is reviewed line by line, this line most of all.

The draft above is a placeholder proving the slot exists. It says only where, when, and that the burial was at night at her own request. Even that last clause is an implication, and the scholar may want it gone.

**No signature, no print.** This one line blocks the entire print run of fourteen. Start that conversation in Phase 0, not at Gate 3.
