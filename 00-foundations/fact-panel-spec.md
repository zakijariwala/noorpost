# Fact panel spec

The reverse of the letter. Story on the front, facts on the back, one sheet.

**Read by the adult, after the letter, out loud or not.** It is where the envelope keeps its promises about accuracy, about the woman in every issue, and about the one new thing.

---

## Skeleton

Fixed order. Same seven slots, same sequence, all fourteen. A parent who has read three of these should be able to find the year without looking.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  [NAME]                                 │  1  Name and dates
│  [b. year AH · d. year AH]              │
│                                         │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                         │
│  ● [Where and when]                     │  2  Place and time
│  ● [Who was ruling]                     │  3  The ruler of the day
│  ● [The imamate]                        │  4  Length, and from what age
│  ● [What he left]                        │  5  Students, compilations, work
│  ● [The woman]                          │  6  Fixed slot
│  ● [Elsewhere in the world]             │  7  Same year, another place
│                                         │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────  │
│                                         │
│  [The death line]                       │  8  Last. Always last.
│                                         │
│  [source credit, 6pt]                   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Six bullets. Not five, not seven. The panel is a fixed shape and a parent learns it by envelope three.

---

## The slots

### 1. Name and dates
Full form per `standard-lines.md`. Birth and death in AH. Where a year is disputed in the sources, print the one on the citation sheet and let the parent's copy carry the variance — not the headline.

### 2. Where and when
One sentence. City and era. This is the slot that stops a child thinking all fourteen lived in the same place at the same time, which most of them assume until told otherwise.

### 3. The ruler of the day
Named. This is the single highest-value bullet in the panel and it does most of the work of the through-question. A child who reads across fourteen envelopes and sees the names of the rulers change while the family does not has understood the shape of the thing without anyone explaining it.

Name the dynasty too, once the caliph is named: *the Umayyad caliph Hisham*, *the Abbasid caliph al-Mansur*.

### 4. The imamate
How long, and from what age. Both numbers. Envelope 14 lives or dies on this bullet — *Imam at about eight years old, for seventeen years* is the whole pitch of the issue.

For envelope 03, the slot instead carries the twenty-three years of revelation. For envelope 06, it carries her lifetime and nothing else.

### 5. What he left
Named students. Attributed compilations. Work that survives and is pointed at, not summarised. Envelope 12 has the most to put here by a wide margin — use it, name six students, name where they went.

**No adjectives in this slot.** *Hundreds of students, including Abu Hanifa and Malik ibn Anas* is a fact. *An enormous influence on Islamic thought* is not, and does not survive checking.

### 6. The woman
**Fixed slot, every envelope, no exceptions.** Assignments in PRD §9 and repeated in `citation-sheet.md`.

She gets a full sentence and something she did, not a relationship. *Khadija funded the early community out of her own trade* passes. *Khadija, the wife of the Prophet* fails — it is a position, not an act.

Where the account is traditional rather than documented, mark it per `standard-lines.md`.

### 7. Elsewhere in the world
The same year, somewhere else. This is the bullet that makes the fourteen sit in real time rather than in a devotional register, and it is the one children read out to their parents unprompted.

Requirements: verifiable, dated to within a decade, and not in the Islamic world. Prefer things a child can picture — a building going up, a book being written, a city being founded.

**Check the arithmetic every time.** AH to CE conversion is the most likely factual error in the whole product because it is the only place doing sums. Use the conversion table in `citation-sheet.md`, and record both years in the working file.

### 8. The death line
Last line. Nowhere else in the envelope. Approved wordings in `death-lines.md`. Not drafted per envelope — the fourteen were written together, on purpose.

## Typography

| Element | Setting |
|---|---|
| Name | Display face, large |
| Dates | Body face, small caps |
| Bullets | Body face, ragged right, one to two lines each |
| The death line | Body face, set apart by space above, no rule, no bold |
| Source credit | 6pt, foot, right |

**The death line is not emphasised.** No bold, no box, no colour. It sits at the foot in the same weight as everything else. Emphasis is a way of building to it, and the envelope does not build to it.

---

## What the panel never contains

- A moral, a lesson, or an application
- A second death, including anyone else's
- Anything from the letter, restated
- Numbers that cannot be checked — followers, converts, people influenced, populations
- Superlatives of any kind
